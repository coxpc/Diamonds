Phase 1 — Warm-Up
0:00–0:15 · 15 min · All 12
▾

Fixed every week. Same sequence, same energy. Get their feet warm — everything else follows from there.

  • Jog + High Knees + Butt Kicks — 2 laps of the 70×40 space. Calf-to-quad progression, nothing skipped.
  • Arm Circles + Shoulder Activation — forward/backward, internal/external rotation. Shoulder health before any throw or swing.
  • Lateral Shuffles + Drop Steps + 10 Dry Swings — hips first, then close with dry swings. Prime the pattern before they ever see a ball.

D1 Situational Live BP — Count Awareness Hitting

Coach Mo pitches from Lane 1 the entire session. Every at-bat starts 1-1. Before each pitch, Mo calls the count out loud. Girls must decide — hunt fastball, protect, or go off-speed — before the ball leaves his hand. No mindless hacks.

This is the tournament prep rep. The goal is a decision, not just contact. Each girl takes 8–10 pitches, steps out, and the next girl steps in immediately.

Coach Mo Cue "What's your job right now?" — ask it before every single pitch. If she can't answer, the pitch doesn't happen.
D2 Load & Launch Off Bounce — Timing Trigger Hitting

Lane 2. Partner drops tennis ball from shoulder height; hitter loads on the drop, fires hips on the bounce. The bounce is the changeup — unpredictable timing forces them to wait back rather than muscle through early.

Think of it like a visual metronome they can't cheat: instead of syncing to a beat, they're syncing to a cue that moves differently every rep. Partner inconsistency actually helps — it mimics real pitch variety.

Self-Coaching Cue "Sit and wait — let the ball come to me." Hands stay back until hips have already started.
D3 Inside-Out Contact (Tee) — No More Casting Hitting

Tees A, B, C — inside pitch location only. Coach Darian floats and corrects. Job is to drive the ball to the opposite gap. Knob leads, barrel follows. Two girls per tee: one hitting, one calling "inside-out" or "cast" on each rep.

Coach Darian Cue "Knob to the ball, not barrel to the ball." If you can see the barrel before contact, it's too early.

D4 Two-Strike Approach — Live BP Hitting

Same live BP format — Coach Mo, 8–10 pitches — but every at-bat starts 0-2. The mental frame shifts completely. No longer hunting; now surviving. Shorten the swing, widen the zone, put the ball in play. Tournament at-bats don't always start in hitter's counts — this closes the gap between practice swings and pressure swings.

Coach Mo Cue "You're not trying to hit a homer. You're trying to make the pitcher make another throw." Survival is the win here.
D5 Dropped Ball Extension Finish Hitting

Partner drops tennis ball; hitter focuses entirely on extension through contact and a full, balanced finish. After a two-strike session of choking up and protecting, they need to remember what full extension feels like. Alternating between short (two-strike) and full (this drill) in the same cage block builds both muscle memories without erasing either.

Self-Coaching Cue "Finish tall." If you're bent at the waist at the finish, the barrel quit early.
D6 High / Low Location Adjust (Tee) Hitting

Tee partner alternates the height — one rep high, one rep low — without telling the hitter in advance. Hitter must read and adjust stance and contact point. Builds barrel control that survives pitch-to-pitch variation in a tournament. Coach Darian corrects posture collapse on low pitches.

Coach Darian Cue "Hips lead regardless of height. Low pitch means longer path, not a dropped chin."
Phase 4 — Bunting (Drill 3 / Transition)
1:07–1:15 · 8 min · Both Groups
▾
D7 Sacrifice + Push Bunt — Game Situation Attached Bunting

All 12 together. Always Drill 3 — the bridge from cages to field. This week's game situation: runner on second, no outs. Half the girls practice the sacrifice; half practice the push bunt to the third-base side to make the third baseman field it. Two reps each, then flip.

In a tournament you might only get one clean shot at this all weekend. Run it like it counts.

Both Coaches "Angle the barrel before the pitch comes. You don't have time to set up mid-delivery." Eyes on the top half of the ball.
Phase 5 — Fielding Fundamentals
1:15–1:35 · 20 min · Split 6+6
▾

Split the 12 into two groups of 6. One coach per group. 70×40 space — cones as bases at 40 feet.

Group A — Coach Darian
Rapid-Fire Grounders (Footwork First)

High-rep grounders. Call footwork corrections before glove corrections. Every time.

Group B — Coach Mo
Short Hop Partner Drill

20+ reps each. Partners 20 feet apart, throwing short hops intentionally. Hands soft, feet set before the catch.

D8 Rapid-Fire Grounders — Footwork First Fielding

Coach rolls/throws grounders in rapid succession. Between each, she resets her feet. Rule: if your feet aren't set when the ball gets to you, that rep doesn't count. Target 20+ reps per girl in 10 minutes. Focus this week on crossover step and charge angle — too many girls are catching balls at their feet instead of out in front.

Coach Darian Cue "Feet first, then glove. Always. If your feet are wrong, your glove can't save you."
D9 Short Hop Partner Drill — Soft Hands Fielding

Partners 20 feet apart. Thrower intentionally creates a short hop — no easy catches. Fielder works: set feet before the ball arrives, give with the hands at contact, frame the catch cleanly. 20+ reps each. Switch roles at the halfway point.

This is scar-tissue work. Short hops are where tournament errors happen because girls panic and go stiff. Volume here builds the reflex so the hands go soft automatically.

Coach Mo Cue "Soft hands means giving, not grabbing. Reach toward it, then let the ball come to you."
Phase 6 — Game Situation IQ
1:35–2:00 · 25 min · All 12
▾

All 12 together. Format: "What Do You Do?" Quiz + Walk-Through. Coach calls a situation, girls answer, then walk it on the field with cones so the read lives in their feet — not just their heads. Tournament-relevant scenarios only.

D10 "What Do You Do?" — Tournament Scenario Quiz Game IQ

Coach calls a situation out loud. All 12 girls answer (thumbs up/down, hand signal, or shout). Walk through 5–6 scenarios at game speed. Discuss the "why," not just the "what." Close every practice with this.

1Runner on 1st, 1 out. Hard grounder to SS. What does 2B do?
Answer: Cover 2B immediately, receive the throw for the force. Don't watch the play — move. Common mistake: girls stand and watch instead of reacting to their assignment.
2Runners on 1st and 3rd, 1 out. Short fly ball to LF drops in. Where does the ball go?
Answer: Runner on 3rd scores. LF throws to 2B cutoff, hold the runner from 1st at 2nd. Throwing home late after the run scores is a free base — don't do it.
3Runner on 2nd, no outs. Pop-up in shallow CF. Two fielders can make it. Who calls it?
Answer: CF has priority on all outfield pop-ups. SS/2B yield unless CF can't reach. The call must be loud and early: "I got it! I got it!" Silence = crash.
40-2 count, runner stealing 2nd. Pitch is in the dirt. What does the runner on 2nd do?
Answer: Stay — don't steal 3rd on a passed ball with two strikes. The defense is expecting aggression. Wait for the clean opportunity.
5You're at SS. Runner on 3rd, 1 out, infield in. Hard shot to you. Where does it go?
Answer: Check the runner — if she hasn't broken, throw to 1B for the out. If she breaks early and you can beat her, go home. The read must be instant: feet tell you before your eyes do.
6Fly ball to RF, runner on 1st tagging up. Where does CF go?
Answer: CF backs up RF. Every outfield ball — someone backs up. No exceptions. If RF drops it, you're the difference between one base and a disaster.
Close Every Practice With This Last thing they hear before they walk out: "You solved it." Name one scenario, one girl who read it right. Specific praise, specific rep. They'll replay it on the drive home — that's the point.
